# RF Site Survey Methodology (Predictive + On-Site)

## CCNP Enterprise (ENCOR 350-401) Wireless Design

**CCNA Relevance:** Basic wireless planning and RF concepts.  
**CCNP ENCOR Relevance:** High — RF site surveys are critical for designing enterprise Wi-Fi networks. Understanding predictive surveys, on-site validation, RF optimization, and troubleshooting is a core wireless design skill.

---

# 1. Overview

An **RF (Radio Frequency) Site Survey** is the process of planning, measuring, and validating a wireless network to ensure:

- Adequate coverage
- Sufficient capacity
- Reliable roaming
- Minimal interference
- Optimal performance

A proper site survey answers:

- **Where should APs be placed?**
- **How many APs are required?**
- **What transmit power should be used?**
- **Which channels should be assigned?**

---

# 2. Why Perform a Site Survey?

Without a survey, organizations may experience:

- Coverage holes
- Dead zones
- Poor roaming
- Channel interference
- Slow throughput
- Excessive retransmissions
- Client disconnects

A site survey reduces these risks before and after deployment.

# 7. RF Obstacles

Different materials attenuate RF signals differently.

| Material | RF Impact |
|----------|-----------|
| Drywall | Low |
| Glass | Low to Moderate |
| Brick | Moderate |
| Concrete | High |
| Metal | Very High |
| Elevator Shaft | Very High |
| Water (people, tanks) | High |

# 8. Predictive Survey

A **Predictive Survey** uses software to simulate RF coverage before AP installation.

```
Floor Plan

↓

Survey Software

↓

Simulated Coverage

↓

Recommended AP Placement
```

No physical measurements are taken at this stage.

---

# 9. Predictive Survey Inputs

Typical inputs:

- Floor plan
- Building materials
- Ceiling height
- AP model
- Antenna type
- Expected user density
- Power levels
- Channel widths

---

# 10. Predictive Survey Output

The software estimates:

- AP locations
- Coverage areas
- Signal strength
- Channel overlap
- Capacity
- Expected roaming

---

# 11. Predictive Survey Advantages

- Fast
- Cost-effective
- No hardware required
- Supports design planning
- Reduces installation errors

---

# 12. Predictive Survey Limitations

It cannot accurately predict:

- Temporary interference
- Unexpected reflections
- Furniture placement
- Human density
- Environmental changes

Therefore, validation is still required.

---

# 14. Coverage Design

Coverage should overlap enough to support seamless roaming.

```
AP 1

(((( Coverage ))))

        Overlap

(((( Coverage ))))

AP 2
```

Too little overlap creates dead zones; too much overlap increases interference.

---

# 15. Capacity Design

Coverage alone is insufficient.

Example:

```
Conference Room

↓

250 Users

↓

Additional APs Needed
```

High-density areas often require more APs even if signal coverage is already adequate.

# 16. Passive Survey

A **Passive Survey** measures the existing RF environment without generating significant client traffic.

The survey device listens to wireless transmissions.

---

# 17. Passive Survey Measures

Examples:

- RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indicator)
- Noise Level
- SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio)
- Channel utilization
- AP visibility
- Neighboring networks

---

# 18. RSSI

RSSI measures received signal strength.

Typical values:

| RSSI | Quality |
|------|---------|
| -30 dBm | Excellent |
| -50 dBm | Very Good |
| -67 dBm | Good (common design target for data/voice) |
| -75 dBm | Fair |
| -85 dBm | Poor |

Closer to **0 dBm** indicates a stronger signal.

---

# 19. SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio)

```
Signal

↓

Noise

↓

Difference

↓

SNR
```

Higher SNR generally provides better communication quality.

Typical targets:

- > 25 dB for general data
- > 30 dB preferred for voice and real-time applications

---

# 20. Noise Floor

Typical enterprise noise floor:

```
-90 dBm

↓

Low Noise

↓

Good
```

A higher (less negative) noise floor can reduce wireless performance.

---

# 26. Interference Types

## Co-Channel Interference (CCI)

Two APs use the **same channel**.

```
AP

Channel 36

↓

AP

Channel 36
```

Clients must share airtime, reducing efficiency.

---

## Adjacent Channel Interference (ACI)

Nearby APs use **overlapping channels**.

```
Channel 4

↓

Channel 5
```

This causes overlapping RF energy and should be avoided.

# 30. Site Survey Best Practices

- Perform a predictive survey before installation.
- Validate with passive and active surveys after deployment.
- Design for both coverage **and** capacity.
- Minimize co-channel interference.
- Use proper channel planning.
- Verify roaming performance.
- Survey after major building changes.
- Document AP locations and RF measurements.
- Repeat surveys periodically as environments change.

# CCNP Exam Traps

- **Predictive surveys** use software simulations and occur **before installation**.
- **Passive surveys** measure the RF environment without generating significant traffic.
- **Active surveys** connect to the WLAN and measure real client performance.
- **Spectrum analysis** identifies **non-Wi-Fi interference**, not just Wi-Fi networks.
- Site surveys must consider both **coverage** and **capacity**.
- **RSSI** measures received signal strength, while **SNR** measures signal quality relative to noise.
- Excessive AP transmit power can **reduce roaming efficiency** and **increase co-channel interference**.
- On **2.4 GHz**, channels **1, 6, and 11** are the preferred non-overlapping channels in most regulatory domains.
