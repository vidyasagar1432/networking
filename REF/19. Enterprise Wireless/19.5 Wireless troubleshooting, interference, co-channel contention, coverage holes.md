# Wireless Troubleshooting: Interference, Co-Channel Contention, and Coverage Holes

## CCNP Enterprise (ENCOR 350-401) Wireless Troubleshooting

**CCNA Relevance:** Basic Wi-Fi troubleshooting concepts.  
**CCNP ENCOR Relevance:** High — Enterprise wireless troubleshooting requires identifying RF interference, co-channel contention (CCI), adjacent-channel interference (ACI), coverage holes, roaming issues, client behavior, and performance bottlenecks.

---

# 1. Overview

Wireless problems are often caused by **RF (Radio Frequency)** issues rather than configuration errors.

Common symptoms include:

- Slow Wi-Fi
- High latency
- Packet loss
- Roaming failures
- Voice call drops
- Client disconnects
- Low throughput

A structured troubleshooting approach helps identify the root cause.

# 6. RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indicator)

Measures received signal strength.

Typical values:

| RSSI | Quality |
|------|---------|
| -30 dBm | Excellent |
| -50 dBm | Very Good |
| -67 dBm | Good |
| -75 dBm | Fair |
| -85 dBm | Poor |

Lower (more negative) values indicate weaker signals.

---

# 7. SNR (Signal-to-Noise Ratio)

```
Signal

↓

Noise

↓

Difference

↓

SNR
```

Typical targets:

| SNR | Quality |
|------|----------|
| >40 dB | Excellent |
| 30–40 dB | Very Good |
| 20–30 dB | Acceptable |
| <20 dB | Poor |

Low SNR often leads to retransmissions and reduced throughput.

---

# 8. Noise Floor


A higher (less negative) noise floor means more RF interference.

Two major categories:

```

```

# 10. Wi-Fi Interference

Examples:

- Neighboring APs
- Overlapping channels
- Dense deployments
- Improper channel planning

---

# 11. Non-Wi-Fi Interference

Examples:

- Microwave ovens
- Bluetooth devices
- Wireless cameras
- Zigbee
- Cordless phones
- Radar (DFS bands)
- Industrial equipment

These devices can interfere with Wi-Fi even though they do not use 802.11.

# 12. Co-Channel Contention (CCI)

**Co-Channel Contention (CCI)** occurs when multiple APs use the **same channel**.

```
AP1

Channel 36

))))))))

Client

((((((((

AP2

Channel 36
```

Unlike ACI, devices on the same channel can understand each other's transmissions and therefore must share airtime using CSMA/CA.

---

# 13. Why CCI Happens

Common causes:

- Too many APs
- Excessive transmit power
- Poor RF planning
- Dense office deployments

---

# 14. Effects of CCI

- Reduced throughput
- Increased latency
- More waiting before transmission
- Lower airtime efficiency
- Slower applications

Because devices share the same channel, overall efficiency decreases as more devices compete for airtime.

---

# 15. CCI Example

```
AP1

↓

Waiting

-------------------

AP2

↓

Transmitting
```

Only one device can successfully transmit on the channel at a given moment.

---

# 16. Adjacent Channel Interference (ACI)

ACI occurs when APs operate on **overlapping channels**.

Example:

```
Channel 4

↓

Overlap

↓

Channel 5
```

Unlike CCI, overlapping channels interfere with each other because they cannot properly coordinate access.

---

# 17. CCI vs ACI

| Feature | CCI | ACI |
|----------|-----|-----|
| Same Channel | Yes | No |
| Overlapping Channels | No | Yes |
| CSMA/CA Works | Yes | No |
| Performance Impact | Moderate to High | Often More Severe |

---

# 18. Avoiding ACI

### 2.4 GHz

Use:

```
1

6

11
```

These are the preferred non-overlapping channels in most regulatory domains.

---

### 5 GHz

Use proper channel planning and Dynamic Channel Assignment (DCA) where supported.

---

### 6 GHz

Provides many more non-overlapping channels, reducing channel overlap.

# 29. Retry Rate

High retry rates indicate packets must be retransmitted.

Common causes:

- Low SNR
- Interference

# 30. Hidden Node Problem

```
Client A

↓

Cannot Hear

↓

Client B

↓

Both Send

↓

Collision at AP
```

Although Wi-Fi uses CSMA/CA, hidden nodes can still cause retransmissions because clients cannot detect each other.

# 31. Capacity vs Coverage

# 35. Best Practices

- Perform predictive and on-site site surveys.
- Use channels **1, 6, and 11** on 2.4 GHz where appropriate.
- Minimize co-channel contention.
- Avoid adjacent-channel interference.
- Design for both coverage and capacity.
- Keep AP transmit power balanced.
- Enable automatic RF optimization features where appropriate.
- Monitor RSSI, SNR, retry rates, and channel utilization continuously.
- Validate roaming with 802.11r/k/v where supported.

# CCNP Exam Traps

- **CCI (Co-Channel Contention)** occurs when APs share the **same channel**; devices coordinate using CSMA/CA but must share airtime.
- **ACI (Adjacent-Channel Interference)** occurs when APs use **overlapping channels** and is typically more harmful than CCI.
- Coverage holes result from **insufficient RF coverage**, not necessarily authentication problems.
- High transmit power can **increase CCI** and **reduce roaming efficiency**.
- A strong **RSSI** does not guarantee good performance—**SNR**, airtime utilization, and interference also matter.
- Hidden nodes increase retransmissions because clients cannot hear one another.
- Spectrum analysis is used to identify **non-Wi-Fi interference** sources.
