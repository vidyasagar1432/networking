# Route Aggregation

> **Level 05 · IPv4** — CCNP Enterprise (ENCOR 350-401)

---

## 1. Overview

**Route aggregation** (also called **route summarization**) represents multiple  
more-specific routes with a single **less-specific prefix**.

The goal is to:

- Reduce routing-table size
    
- Reduce routing-protocol overhead
    
- Simplify routing
    
- Improve scalability
    
- Limit the propagation of individual route changes
    

Aggregation can be performed by **IGPs, BGP, or static routing designs**.

> **Core idea:** Many specific prefixes → one less-specific aggregate.

Example:

```text
10.1.0.0/24
10.1.1.0/24
10.1.2.0/24
10.1.3.0/24
        ↓
10.1.0.0/22
```

The `/22` represents all four `/24` networks.

---

## 2. Core Concepts

### 2.1 Aggregation vs Summarization vs Supernetting

These terms are closely related but are not always interchangeable.

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**Route aggregation**|General technique of representing multiple routes with fewer routes|
|**Route summarization**|Creating a less-specific route that represents multiple more-specific routes|
|**Supernetting**|Combining multiple contiguous networks into a larger prefix|
|**CIDR**|Classless addressing that enables arbitrary prefix lengths and aggregation|
|**LPM**|Forwarding rule that selects the most-specific matching route|

> For CCNP purposes, **route aggregation and route summarization are often used interchangeably** when discussing routing tables.

---

### 2.2 Aggregation Points

Aggregation can occur at different places in the routing architecture.

|Technology|Common Mechanism|
|---|---|
|**OSPF**|`area range`|
|**EIGRP**|`ip summary-address eigrp`|
|**BGP**|`aggregate-address`|
|**Static routing**|Aggregate route, often with `Null0`|
|**RIB/CEF**|Longest-prefix match selects the best matching route|

---

## 3. The Aggregate + Null0 Pattern

A common routing design is to install a **discard route** for the aggregate.

Example:

```text
ip route 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 null0
```

Suppose the router owns:

```text
10.1.0.0/24
10.1.1.0/24
10.1.2.0/24
10.1.3.0/24
```

and advertises:

```text
10.1.0.0/22
```

The `Null0` route provides a safe destination for traffic matching the  
aggregate when no more-specific route exists.

### Why?

Consider:

```text
10.1.0.0/24  → real route
10.1.1.0/24  → real route
10.1.2.0/24  → real route
10.1.3.0/24  → real route
10.1.4.0/24  → outside the /22
```

If a covered prefix is unallocated or unavailable:

```text
10.1.2.100
```

and no `/24` route exists, the router can fall back to:

```text
10.1.0.0/22 → Null0
```

and discard the packet locally rather than forwarding it toward an incorrect  
destination or potentially creating a routing loop.

> **Important:** `Null0` is a common safety mechanism, not a mandatory requirement for every aggregation design.

---

## 4. Longest-Prefix Match (LPM)

When multiple routes match a destination, the router selects the route with the  
**longest prefix length**.

Example:

```text
10.1.0.0/22   → Null0
10.1.2.0/24   → Gi0/1
```

Destination:

```text
10.1.2.50
```

Both routes match, but:

```text
/24 > /22
```

Therefore:

```text
10.1.2.0/24
```

wins.

### Key rule

```text
More-specific route
        ↓
     wins over
        ↓
Less-specific aggregate
```

This is why an aggregate can coexist with its more-specific routes.

> **Exam point:** LPM is a **forwarding decision**, not the process of creating the aggregate.

---

## 5. Building a Valid Aggregate

A summary must correctly cover the intended networks.

Consider:

```text
192.168.0.0/24
192.168.1.0/24
192.168.2.0/24
192.168.3.0/24
```

These four `/24`s can be summarized as:

```text
192.168.0.0/22
```

because `/22` contains exactly four `/24` networks:

```text
192.168.0.0/24
192.168.1.0/24
192.168.2.0/24
192.168.3.0/24
```

### Important requirements

A valid aggregate must have:

1. The correct **prefix length**
    
2. The correct **network boundary/alignment**
    
3. Coverage of all intended networks
    
4. No unintended address space, unless that is deliberately acceptable
    

> **Exam trap:** Do not assume that any numerically adjacent networks can be summarized into any desired prefix. **Binary alignment matters.**

## 6. BGP Route Aggregation

BGP supports aggregation using:

```text
aggregate-address
```

Example:

```text
router bgp 65001
 aggregate-address 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0
```

This creates the aggregate:

```text
10.1.0.0/22
```

when appropriate more-specific routes exist in the BGP table.

### 6.1 Keeping the Specific Routes

By default, BGP aggregation can advertise the aggregate while the  
more-specific routes may continue to be advertised.

Conceptually:

```text
10.1.0.0/24
10.1.1.0/24
10.1.2.0/24
10.1.3.0/24
        ↓
10.1.0.0/22
```

The aggregate and specifics can both be present.

---

### 6.2 `summary-only`

Use:

```text
aggregate-address 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 summary-only
```

`summary-only` suppresses the covered more-specific routes from being  
advertised to the relevant BGP peers while advertising the aggregate.

Conceptually:

```text
Without summary-only:

10.1.0.0/22  ─┐
10.1.0.0/24  ─┤
10.1.1.0/24  ─┤ → BGP peer
10.1.2.0/24  ─┤
10.1.3.0/24  ─┘

With summary-only:

10.1.0.0/22  → BGP peer
```

> **Exam trap:** `summary-only` controls advertisement of the covered specifics; it does not mean the more-specific routes cease to exist locally.

---

## 7. BGP Attribute Considerations

When BGP creates an aggregate, attributes of the aggregate require careful  
consideration.

BGP can use mechanisms such as:

- `as-set`
    
- `attribute-map`
    
- `route-map`
    
- `suppress-map`
    

depending on the desired policy.

Example:

```text
aggregate-address 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 as-set
```

`as-set` can preserve contributing AS-path information in the aggregate's  
AS_PATH.

> **Exam focus:** Know that aggregation can change the information available  
> for path selection because multiple specific routes are represented by one  
> less-specific route.

## 8. IGP Summarization

### 8.1 OSPF

OSPF supports summarization at specific boundaries.

For example:

```text
area 1 range 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0
```

This can summarize multiple networks from an area into:

```text
10.1.0.0/22
```

OSPF summarization is commonly associated with:

- **ABRs** — inter-area summarization
    
- **ASBRs** — summarization of external routes
    

---

### 8.2 EIGRP

EIGRP supports interface-based manual summarization:

```text
router eigrp 100
 interface GigabitEthernet0/1
  ip summary-address eigrp 100 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0
```

The summary is advertised out of that interface.

> **Important:** EIGRP summarization is configured on the interface where the  
> summary should be advertised.

---

## 9. Static Route Aggregation

A static aggregate can also be used:

```text
ip route 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 Null0
```

This is especially useful when a router needs to:

- Originate an aggregate
    
- Provide a discard path for unmatched destinations
    
- Prevent forwarding loops caused by an aggregate
    

A more-specific route still wins:

```text
10.1.2.0/24 → actual next hop
10.1.0.0/22 → Null0
```

Destination:

```text
10.1.2.50
```

uses the `/24`, not `Null0`.

---

## 10. Aggregation and Route Leaking

Aggregation hides more-specific routing information from parts of the network.

Example:

```text
Before summarization:

10.1.0.0/24
10.1.1.0/24
10.1.2.0/24
10.1.3.0/24

After summarization:

10.1.0.0/22
```

A remote router now sees only:

```text
10.1.0.0/22
```

### Benefit

Smaller routing tables and less routing information.

### Trade-off

The remote router may lose knowledge of the individual paths to the  
more-specific networks.

For example, suppose:

```text
10.1.2.0/24 → Path A
10.1.3.0/24 → Path B
```

After aggregation, a remote router may only know:

```text
10.1.0.0/22 → Path A
```

It can no longer independently select the better path for every covered  
prefix.

### Route leaking

When a specific route must still be exposed across a summarization boundary,  
the more-specific route can be intentionally **leaked** or advertised.

> **Exam idea:** Summarization reduces information; route leaking selectively restores required information.

---

## 11. Aggregation and Routing Loops

A poorly designed aggregate can create forwarding problems.

Example:

```text
       Core
        |
     10.1.0.0/22
        |
   Distributor
      /   \
   LAN-A  LAN-B
```

The distributor advertises:

```text
10.1.0.0/22
```

If it does not have a real route for a particular destination inside the  
summary, forwarding the packet toward another router that also advertises or  
uses the same summary can potentially create a loop.

Installing:

```text
ip route 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 Null0
```

provides a local discard path.

More-specific routes remain preferred because of LPM.

---

## 12. Route Aggregation vs Longest-Prefix Match

These concepts are related but different.

|Concept|Purpose|
|---|---|
|**Aggregation**|Combines multiple routes into a less-specific route|
|**Summarization**|Represents multiple prefixes with one summary|
|**LPM**|Selects the most-specific matching route|
|**Null0**|Discards traffic that matches an aggregate but has no valid specific route|

Example:

```text
10.1.0.0/22 → Null0
10.1.2.0/24 → Gi0/1
```

For:

```text
10.1.2.10
```

LPM selects:

```text
10.1.2.0/24
```

For:

```text
10.1.1.10
```

if no `/24` exists, the `/22` may be selected:

```text
10.1.0.0/22 → Null0
```

### EIGRP Summary

```text
router eigrp 100
 interface GigabitEthernet0/1
  ip summary-address eigrp 100 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0
```

### OSPF Area Summary

```text
router ospf 1
 area 1 range 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0
```

### BGP Aggregate

```text
router bgp 65001
 neighbor 10.0.0.2 remote-as 65002
 aggregate-address 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0
```

### BGP Aggregate with `summary-only`

```text
router bgp 65001
 neighbor 10.0.0.2 remote-as 65002
 aggregate-address 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 summary-only
```

### Null0 Safety Route

```text
ip route 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 Null0
```


## 14. Exam Traps

### Trap 1 — LPM vs Aggregation

**Wrong:** "The aggregate wins because it was configured first."

**Correct:** The **longest matching prefix** wins.

```text
/24 beats /22
/27 beats /24
/32 beats /27
```

---

### Trap 2 — Null0 Is Not Always Mandatory

Do not memorize:

> "Every summary must have a Null0 route."

Instead remember:

> **A Null0 route is a common safety mechanism for an advertised/locally  
> installed aggregate to prevent unwanted forwarding of destinations for which  
> no more-specific route exists.**

---

### Trap 3 — Alignment Matters

Do not summarize prefixes merely because they appear sequential.

The aggregate must be correctly aligned on the appropriate binary boundary.

---

### Trap 4 — `summary-only`

```text
aggregate-address 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0
```

and:

```text
aggregate-address 10.1.0.0 255.255.252.0 summary-only
```

are not equivalent.

`summary-only` suppresses the covered more-specific routes from being  
advertised by BGP while the aggregate is advertised.

---

### Trap 5 — Aggregation Does Not Mean the Specifics Disappear Everywhere

A router may advertise only:

```text
10.1.0.0/22
```

while another router may still possess:

```text
10.1.0.0/24
10.1.1.0/24
10.1.2.0/24
10.1.3.0/24
```

Aggregation affects **route advertisement and visibility**, not necessarily  
the existence of the underlying routes.

---

### Trap 6 — Aggregation Can Reduce Path Visibility

A summary hides individual paths.

Therefore, aggregation improves scalability but can reduce routing-policy  
granularity and path-selection visibility.

## 15. Quick Review Table

|Item|Key Point|
|---|---|
|**What**|Multiple routes → fewer routes|
|**Goal**|Smaller routing tables and better scalability|
|**IGP**|OSPF `area range`, EIGRP `ip summary-address`|
|**BGP**|`aggregate-address`|
|**BGP suppression**|`summary-only`|
|**Forwarding**|Longest-prefix match|
|**Safety**|`Null0` can discard unmatched traffic within an aggregate|
|**Requirement**|Correct prefix length and binary alignment|
|**Specific routes**|May remain locally even when not advertised|
|**Trade-off**|Less path visibility / less granular routing policy|
|**Leak**|Re-advertise selected specifics when necessary|
|**Use case**|Enterprise boundaries, IGP areas, ISP/BGP networks|
|**Main benefit**|Scalability and reduced routing information|
