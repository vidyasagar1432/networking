# Wireless (RF as a Cable)

> **Level 01 · 01. Cables** — CCNP Enterprise (ENCOR 350-401)

---

## 1. Overview

"Wireless" replaces a physical cable with **radio-frequency (RF) electromagnetic
signals**. Covered at the physical layer for how it differs from copper/fiber:
shared medium, over-the-air loss, interference, and regulatory spectrum.
(Full WLAN/L2–L7 story: Level 22 Wireless.)

---

## 2. Core Concepts

### 2.1 Comparisons vs guided media
| Aspect | Copper / Fiber (guided) | Wireless (unguided RF) |
|--------|-------------------------|------------------------|
| Medium | Cable, private | Air, **shared** with everything |
| Security | Physical access required | Anyone in range can listen |
| Determinism | Predictable | Interference, multipath, fading |
| Distance | Meters–100s km | Depends on path loss & antennas |
| Bandwidth | Very high, stable | Governed by spectrum + modulation |

### 2.2 RF basics (physical-layer vocabulary)
- **Frequency bands:** 2.4 GHz (ISM, 11 channels), 5 GHz, 6 GHz (Wi‑Fi 6E).
- **Channel width** (20/40/80/160 MHz) trades speed for available spectrum.
- **Power**: EIRP = transmit power + antenna gain − cable/connector loss.
- **Path loss** grows with distance and frequency; **multipath** (reflections) causes
  fading and intersymbol interference; **fading** can be fast (moving) or slow.

### 2.3 Interference and coexistence
- Same-band non-WiFi devices compete (Bluetooth, Zigbee, microwave at 2.4 GHz).
- **Co-channel interference** (same channel nearby) and adjacent-channel.
- Channel selection: use non-overlapping **1, 6, 11** on 2.4 GHz; DFS channels exist on
  5 GHz (radar avoidance).

### 2.4 Summary of where RF = "cable"
- Client ↔ AP link is the "radio cable"; AP attaches to the wired campus via a standard
  copper/fiber uplink (and CAPWAP tunnel to WLC, Level 22).
- Good practice: wireless design starts with **RF survey** (heatmaps), channel plan,
  power budget; the exam tests the concept of shared medium + interference above all.

## 4. Exam Traps

- **Wireless shares the medium**: unlike switched Ethernet, air cannot be "switched" —
  each AP cell is one collision domain at RF.
- **Interference (RF) vs congestion (capacity)** are different problems — both appear
  in troubleshooting scenarios.
- **2.4 GHz channels 1/6/11** only non-overlapping; never pick adjacent channels.
- **EIRP and path loss** explain why a far user has poor quality — signal, not the
  IP config, is the first suspect in physical-layer wireless troubleshooting.
- **RF ≠ "wireless everything"**: actual user traffic reaches clients over the radio
  *and* travels the wired LAN to the controller/gateway.
