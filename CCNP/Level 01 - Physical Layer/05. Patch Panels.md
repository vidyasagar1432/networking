# Patch Panels

> **Level 01 · Physical Layer** — CCNP Enterprise (ENCOR 350-401)

---

## 1. Overview

**Patch panels** are the structured-cabling termination point where horizontal/riser
cables are landed and then **patched** to active equipment with short jumper cables.
They enable moves, adds, and changes (MAC) without disturbing permanent cabling, and
keep equipment clean and serviceable.

---

## 2. Core Concepts

### 2.1 Why panels exist
- **Termination point** for UTP/STP/fiber before equipment.
- **Centralization** at the distribution frame (IDF/ER) and data center pod.
- **Changeability** — patch a new user port to a different switch without pulling cable.
- **Protection** of the more fragile permanent link; panels define the "90 m + 10 m" budget split.

### 2.2 Types

| Panel | For | Notes |
|-------|-----|-------|
| RJ-45 copper modular | Cat5e/6/6a UTP | Keystone jacks A/B colors |
| Fiber LC panel | LC duplex runs | adapters per port |
| Fiber SC/MPO panel | MPO backbone / 40/100G | breakout trunks |
| **MTP/MPO breakout** | high-density DC | 12/24 to LC fan-out |
| Blank/keystone | mixed use | count RU |

### 2.3 Cabling terms with panels
- **Horizontal cable** (from panel to wall outlet in the workspace) ≤ 90 m.
- **Patch cables** (panel→switch, wall→device) sum ≤ 10 m.
- **Backbone/riser** uplink to the dist/core in a separate frame (MDF/IDF).

### 2.4 Design rules
- **Always label** — port maps are mandatory for ops.
- **Auto-config/documentation**: maintain a cable database; or modern: use LLDP/CDP to
  auto-discover the *logical* neighbor behind each patch (but the panel is physical).
- **Fiber polarity through two panels** must flip, or a loop occurs.
- **Leave spare NLUs/horizon** so future add-ons don't re-wire.

## 3. Configuration / Commands

```text
# No commands — hardware. But document neighborhood via CDP/LLDP:
show cdp neighbors detail
show lldp neighbors detail
```

*Before replacing a suspected "bad port" at a switch, you physically swap the
patch cord at the panel — one of the first L1 steps (see Level 29 Layer 1).*

---

## 4. Exam Traps

- **Panels don't "make" the network choose path** — they are L1 terminations; CDP/LLDP
  neighbors may differ from the panel map (that's a maintenance curiosity, not a failure).
- **Polarity (fiber)** must be maintained across panels — flipping does TX→RX.
- **A bad patch = a bad link**, indistinguishable at L1 from other media errors until
  you inspect the specific port.
- **Panel ≠ active device**: no MAC, no IP.
