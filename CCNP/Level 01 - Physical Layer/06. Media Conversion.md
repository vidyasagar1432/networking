# Media Conversion

> **Level 01 · Physical Layer** — CCNP Enterprise (ENCOR 350-401)

---

## 1. Overview

**Media conversion** changes a signal between different physical media — most commonly
between **copper Ethernet (100BASE-TX/1000BASE-T)** and **fiber optics**, or between
electrical and optical realms generally. The conversion is transparent to L2/L3: the
frame bytes are unchanged.

---

## 2. Core Concepts

### 2.1 What a converter does
- **Electrical ↔ optical**: convert between electrical Manchester/null encoding (PCS)
  and optical on/off; the Ethernet frame itself is untouched.
- May re-clock/regenerate (a **repeater**), but today most are **transparent switches**
  (L1 conversion inside standalone "media converter").

### 2.2 Where used
- **Extending distance**: UTP limit 100 m → fiber (multi/single-mode) for 550 m–10 km.
- **EMI environments** where copper is impossible (industrial, between buildings where
  fiber is the run).
- **Different optics** (MM↔SM) in patch panels or switches (mode-converters count).
- **Legacy links** (10/100) vs new (gigabit) — replaces NIC/card changes at ends.

### 2.3 Types

| Conversion | From | To | Notes |
|------------|------|----|-------|
| 100BASE-TX→100BASE-FX | RJ45 | ST/SC duplex | Old classic |
| 1000BASE-T↔1000BASE-X | RJ45 | LC SFP or standalone module | Often inside SFP copper |
| 10GBASE-T→10GBASE-LR | RJ45 (Cat6a) | LC SM | Limited; latency moderate |
| MM↔SM (mode-conditioning) | MMF 62.5 | SM pigtail | 1000BASE-LX with mode conditioning |
| PoE conversion | UTP power | fiber + injector | separate power consideration |

### 2.4 Key properties / caveats
- **Transparent**: L2 MAC stays; no change to MTU/frames.
- **Latency** added (µs) — fine for campus, but count it in long chains.
- **PoE passthrough**: fiber has no power — devices behind media converters need their own
  power/PoE injector — this is the forgotten design detail.
- **Do not cascade unlimitedly**: regeneration/re-timing can accumulate bit errors; designs keep short.
- Direction: **media converter ≠ router** — L1↔L2 only; no VLAN/ACL logic.

## 3. Configuration / Commands

```text
# Copper SFP is itself a media converter inside the switch port
interface GigabitEthernet0/12

 duplex full

# Verify media/type (fiber SFP vs copper)
show interface gi0/12
show interface transceiver | include "(Type|Media|Speed)"
```

---

## 4. Exam Traps

- **Converter ≠ switch**: no MAC learning/filtering, no VLAN (mostly) — a L1 device.
- **Fiber link → PoE device behind it needs separate power.** Classic design gap.
- **MM↔SM converges badly** without mode-conversion (use LX/CWDMs properly) —
  know the 1000BASE-LX "mode-conditioning patch" story.
- **Cascading converters** accumulate latency/errors; avoid stacking.
- **Speed must match** on both sides (100 vs 1000 vs 10G) — a converter that re-negotiates
  mismatches silently caps the link.
