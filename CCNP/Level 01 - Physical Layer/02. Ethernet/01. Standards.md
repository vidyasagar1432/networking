# Ethernet Standards

> **Level 01 · 02. Ethernet** — CCNP Enterprise (ENCOR 350-401)

---

## 1. Overview

Ethernet (IEEE 802.3) is the de facto LAN standard: from 10 Mbps shared hubs to
400 Gbps switched fabrics. Knowing the **naming scheme** for standards translates
the physical encoding, media, and reach at a glance.

---

## 2. Core Concepts

### 2.1 802.3 naming convention
`<speed><BASE or 10GBASE><media/modifier>`

- **BASE** = baseband (single carrier channel).
- Media letters:
  - **T** = twisted pair (copper UTP/STP)
  - **S** = short-range multimode (SX, SR)
  - **L** = long-range (LX, LR): for SMF/former multi-mode reach
  - **E** = extended reach (ER)
  - **C** = copper twinax/modifier
  - **K** = backplane (KR)
  - **F** = fiber variants (FX for 100M, and —FP, etc.)
  - Modifiers: **X** = 4B/5B/8B/10B or physical coding variant, **R** = 64B/66B
    encoding; some names use a descriptor (**-T** vs **-S**...).

### 2.2 Key standards table
| Standard | Speed | Media | Max segment (typical) |
|----------|-------|-------|-----------------------|
| 10BASE5 (thicknet) | 10 Mbps | Coax | 500 m |
| 10BASE2 (thinnet) | 10 Mbps | Coax | 185 m |
| 10BASE-T | 10 Mbps | Cat 3+ UTP | 100 m |
| 100BASE-TX | 100 Mbps | Cat 5 UTP (2 pair) | 100 m |
| 100BASE-FX | 100 Mbps | MMF | 2 km (half-duplex) / 412 m – 1 km HD |
| 1000BASE-T | 1 Gbps | Cat 5e (4 pair) | 100 m |
| 1000BASE-SX | 1 Gbps | MM (850 nm) | 220–550 m |
| 1000BASE-LX | 1 Gbps | MM (1300 nm) / SM | 550 m MMF / 5 km SMF |
| 1000BASE-LH | 1 Gbps | SMF | 10 km (proprietary but common) |
| 10GBASE-T | 10 Gbps | Cat 6a | 100 m |
| 10GBASE-SR | 10 Gbps | MM SR | 300 m (OM3) |
| 10GBASE-LR | 10 Gbps | SM LR | 10 km |
| 10GBASE-ER | 10 Gbps | SM | 40 km |
| 40GBASE-LR4 | 40 Gbps | SM (4×CWDM) | 10 km |
| 40GBASE-SR4 | 40 Gbps | MM parallel (MPO) | 100 m (OM3) |
| 100GBASE-LR4 / SR4 | 100 Gbps | SM/MM | 10 km / 100 m |
| 100GBASE-CR4 | 100 Gbps | Twinax DAC | up to ~5 m |

### 2.3 Physical layer roles — the encoding trio
- **PCS (physical coding sublayer)** — encodes/decodes bits (8B/10B, 64B/66B,
  4B/5B with 100BASE-TX) to balance DC and provide line code.
- **PMA (physical medium attachment)** — serializes/deserializes onto the wire.
- **PMD (physical medium dependent)** — defines the actual optics/electronic signals
  (modulation, power levels, and cable choice).
- The **MDI** is the actual connector (RJ45, LC).

### 2.4 Ethernet naming in perspective
10G uses "-R" (64B/66B) classifications: SR, LR, ER, ZR; 40/100G parallel
(SR4, CR4, LR4 WDM). Backplane = -KR. Copper twinax (DAC) = -CR.

## 3. Configuration / Commands

```text
# Set an explicit speed/duplex example
interface GigabitEthernet0/1
 speed nonegotiate        # fiber: no autoneg for 1G-fiber (SX/LX manual)
interface TenGigabitEthernet1/0/1
 duplex full               # 10G (and above) are never half-duplex

# See negotiated speeds across links
show interfaces status
show interfaces counters rate
show etherchannel summary        # for EC member speeds (Level 08)
```

## 4. Exam Traps

- **The "BASE-T" speeds map to copper; "-S/L" map to optics** — be careful with
  1000BASE-T vs 1000BASE-SX.
- **Distance limits** per standard, not "always 100 m": 1000BASE-T=100 m, but
  1000BASE-LX SMF=5 km, 10GBASE-LR=10 km.
- **Different speeds ⇔ different PCS/PMD codes**: 8B/10B (1G), 64B/66B (10G), etc.
  You don't need to memorize encodings, but know that they differ.
- **Half-duplex** exists only for 10/100M variants; 1000BASE-* and above are
  full-duplex only (no CSMA/CD in practice).
- **Multi-mode with 1300 nm (LX) on MMF** requires a **mode-conditioning patch
  cable** to avoid launch conditions issues — a classic design detail.
